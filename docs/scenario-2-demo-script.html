Before you start (2 minutes)

  • Open the demo and click Reset demo in the header. Open three tabs from the sidebar: Order Clubbing (step 1), Routes & Schedules (steps 2 and 4) and Material Allocator (step 3); keep Sales Orders & TDC handy for the consequences.
  • Frame the moment: it is 10:30 on the as-of day and the PPC desk is working the CGL, colour-coating and slitter queues for the next few days. SAP feeds orders, APS sends priority flags, the MES owns the line sequence, the batch allocation and the production orders.
  • Every action is live and stays consistent across pages (kept in the browser). Before the demo day, run python tools/generate_data.py and push, so the dates move to the current date. It is the same dataset as Scenario 1: the orders, coils and lines here are the ones the golden thread runs through.

Click Show before swap, then Apply swap. Two galvanized coils of the identical TDC (DX51D+Z 0.80 × 1250, Z120): GIC-KHP-2609-0012 (20.85 t) was allocated to Vega Consumer Durables 4213090008/10 (100 t, required 15 Sep) and GIC-KHP-2609-0014 (17.51 t) to Pinnacle Sheet Metal 4213090012/10 (80 t, required 9 Sep). PPC swaps them because Pinnacle has the earlier delivery date and needs the heavier coil. The balance-to-allocate moves with the coils: Pinnacle 62.49 → 59.15 t, Vega 79.15 → 82.49 t; balance-to-produce does not change, because nothing new was produced. Both allocation records keep the previous item (ALC-SW-0012, ALC-SW-0014), the coils keep their genealogy, and the batch re-allocation message IF-SAP-15 posted to SAP with status OK. The fulfilment bars on the Sales Orders page follow the same numbers.

Type 0013 in the search box to find coil PPG-KHP-2609-0013 (Vega Consumer Durables) and click Reroute. Keep the option Reroute to Rewinding + Trimming Line and read the impact boxes, then click Apply. The route master lists the Rewinding + Trimming Line as the alternate work centre for slitting and trimming (ten routes carry it), so the planner may move a coil when the slitter is loaded. The impact panel shows the equipment loading change before it is applied, over the 10-day horizon: Slitter 51 h → 50 h, Rewinding + Trimming 13 h → 14 h, and the rework production order PO-KHP-2609-R001 that will be generated automatically while the original PO-KHP-2609-0115 is closed short. If the new window would collide with the coil's next step, that step is shifted too (badge SHIFTED) so the sequence stays valid; the EST check under the gantt stays at zero violations. The KPI now reads 1 · 1 rework PO. Say that the rework order carries the updated route and yield string of the new work centre.

Optional extras (if time allows)

  • Same-line rework. Switch the line selector to Continuous Galvanizing Line, search 0026, click Reroute on the Apex Appliances coil CRF-KHP-2609-0026 and choose Rework — re-process on Continuous Galvanizing Line: rework order PO-KHP-2609-R002 is sequenced after the current campaign and the CGL load over the horizon rises from 181 h to 182 h.
  • Full line load. Scroll back to the gantt section: the full-load view shows the campaign plan on every line, one colour per sales order so a campaign reads as a run of one colour, with loading percentages and the EST precedence check under it; the selected-order view shows only the chosen order across its route steps, one colour per coil so each coil can be followed from pickling to packing, including its campaign coils and any coil beyond the horizon. The Coil Plan page (sidebar, under Planning) lists every coil of that load in sequence with its route as chips and its planned window, with order, line and kind filters; click a row for the operation schedule per step (planned start and end, duration, wait before the step, reroutes and shifts).
  • Operations confirmation. Click Confirm on any open schedule line: the confirmation timestamp appears and the schedule message goes to L2 through the middleware.
  • Where it lands. The Plant Dashboard shows the running batches and the behind-schedule count; the Integration Monitor lists the SAP and L2 interfaces used in this scenario (order feed, batch re-allocation IF-SAP-15, schedule to L2).

Tips

Order of steps. Step 1 has its own page, Order Clubbing, laid out as four cards in the order you talk: feed, pool, proposals, accepted plans. Steps 2 and 4 are on Routes & Schedules, top to bottom: rush card, line schedule. Step 3 is on the Material Allocator. The order of the steps does not matter to the demo state.
Proposal ids are stable. A proposal keeps its id when more orders arrive, so an accepted proposal never changes its label mid-demo.
Timing. Step 1 about 8 min · step 2 about 5 min · step 3 about 7 min · step 4 about 10 min.
Reset between runs. Live actions stay in the browser; click Reset demo before the next audience, otherwise the accepted proposals, the reroutes and the swap state from the last run are still there.

Honesty notes

The clubbing engine is a demo heuristic: first-fit of slit widths into standard mother-coil widths (1000 / 1220 / 1250 / 1500 mm) with 20 mm edge trim, and campaign clubbing of full-width items with an identical spec. The real product would use the planning engine's rules and the customer's mother-coil catalogue.
The APS rush flag and the batch swap are pre-computed in the dataset (before and after sequences, BTA before and after); the buttons replay them. The six leftover orders are simulated in the page and are not part of the JSON data feed for the chatbot.
Rework production orders and reroute times are generated in the browser: the coil is placed after the last open job on the alternate line (plus 30 minutes) or at its original time if that is later. No SAP or L2 round trip happens in the static demo.
All customers, orders, coils and people are fictional; line names, products, rules and the interfaces come from the RFP.
